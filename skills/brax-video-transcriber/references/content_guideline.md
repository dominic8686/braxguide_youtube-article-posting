## STYLE
Brax content follows an informative and authentic editorial style — structured for clarity, trust, and accessibility.
Structure: Articles follow a clear logic — start with the challenge (the problem Big Tech created), move through our philosophy (why we built it), and close with the solution (our product or initiative).
Form: Complete sentences, full paragraphs, descriptive subheadings, and natural transitions.
Depth: We prioritize clarity over hype — every claim is supported by facts, data, or context.
Readability: Aim for a high-school reading level — simple enough for broad audiences, precise enough for experts.
Formatting: Use concise paragraphs, bullet lists for features or technical content, and consistent use of bold and italics for emphasis.
Our writing must feel real, human, and confident, reflecting that Brax is both a product maker and a movement builder.

## TONE
Brax tone adapts slightly depending on the medium, but remains grounded and authentic.
In product or technical content: Precise, practical, and transparent. We explain how things work, why we made specific decisions, and what users can expect.
In announcements: Proud but humble. We celebrate progress as a community achievement, not corporate success.
In educational or thought leadership content: Confident and informative, advocating for user rights and open technology without sounding militant or ideological.
In community communication: Friendly, inclusive, and encouraging.
We avoid hype, buzzwords, or empty marketing language. Instead, we use clarity, sincerity, and detail to build trust.
